# User Persona: Andrei, the University Student

* **Who are they?**
    * Andrei is a 21-year-old, third-year university student majoring in Computer Science. He's currently taking several heavy, theoretical courses like "Data Structures & Algorithms" and "Operating Systems," alongside his "Software Engineering" project.

* **Personalization & Background:**
    * He's tech-savvy and organized, using tools like Notion and Google Drive to manage his notes.
    * He studies both alone and with a small group of 2-3 friends.
    * He gets good grades but feels a lot of "exam anxiety," which comes from the fear of not knowing what to study.

* **Job-related (Academic "Job"):**
    * His "job" is to consume a massive amount of information (lecture slides, PDF textbooks, video transcripts, old exam papers) and be able to apply it to specific exam problems.
    * He finds it extremely difficult to connect a specific exam question (e.g., "How would you optimize this graph traversal?") to the *exact* slide in a 200-page lecture PDF that holds the key.

* **Relevance (Interest in the Product):**
    * Andrei's biggest pain point is the "search" problem. `Ctrl+F` is useless when he doesn't know the exact keyword.
    * He doesn't need another place to chat with friends (he has Discord/WhatsApp). He needs a "smarter search" for his own files.
    * When his study group shares documents, they're scattered across Google Drive, Discord, and email. He wants one "single source of truth" for the group.

* **Scenario (Using the App):**
    1.  Andrei and his study group for "Algorithms" create a "Study Space."
    2.  They upload all their lecture notes (4 PDFs), the textbook (1 PDF), and three past exam papers.
    3.  Andrei gets stuck on a mock exam question: "What is the difference in application between a heap and a hash map for priority queues?"
    4.  He opens the **AI Passage Finder**, pastes in the question, and hits "Search."
    5.  The tool returns 5 passages, sorted by relevance.
        * Result 1: A slide from "Lecture 4" defining Priority Queues.
        * Result 2: A paragraph from the textbook on "Heap Implementation."
        * Result 3: A slide from "Lecture 6" comparing `O(1)` vs `O(log n)` for "Hash Map vs. Heap."
    6.  This is the key! He clicks the "Go to page" link for Result 3 to read the full slide.
    7.  He still doesn't fully get the `O(log n)` part. He highlights the passage in the document viewer and leaves a comment: "@[group_member] is this the part he said would be on the exam?"
    8.  He then copies the textbook's technical definition (Result 2) and opens the **General AI Chatbot** in the app. He types: "Can you explain this in simpler terms: '[pasted textbook passage]...'"
    9.  Andrei is satisfied with the clear explanation from the chatbot. He trusts this entire process because the app's design gave him full control. It didn't provide a single, unverifiable "AI answer." Instead, the Passage Finder first proved it had the correct information by showing him the exact passage from his professor's lecture, complete with the source ("Lecture 6, Page 3"). Only after he verified this source himself did he use the General Chatbot as a tool to explain that specific, trusted text. He feels confident, knowing the foundational information comes directly from his teacher, not a random AI."
